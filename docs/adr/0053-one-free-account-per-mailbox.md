# 53. One free account per mailbox, not per address

Date: 2026-10-29

## Status

Accepted. Builds on [ADR 0049](0049-sign-up-without-a-card.md) (sign-up into the free plan) and
[ADR 0051](0051-an-ended-subscription-falls-back-to-the-free-plan.md) (falling back to free).

## Context

The terms that bring the free plan (1.3, Clause 6.4) allow one free account per person and call
a second one, opened with another address to add up the plan's limits, abuse. The service held
one free account per owner, and an owner is an address: the sign-up only trims and lowercases
it. So `ana+1@gmail.com`, `ana+2@gmail.com` and `a.na@gmail.com`, which all reach one inbox,
were three owners and could hold three free accounts, each with its 10,000 events, without
using a disposable e-mail service at all.

An address cannot prove who a person is, and lists of disposable domains go stale as fast as the
services change domains. What the service can see is the mailbox an address delivers to, when
the provider defines it: almost every provider delivers `local+tag@domain` to `local@domain`,
and Gmail also ignores dots in the local part and treats `googlemail.com` as `gmail.com`.

## Decision

1. **Every admin has a mailbox**, stored as `admin_users.mailbox`, a column generated by
   Postgres from the e-mail (`GENERATED ALWAYS AS … STORED`, indexed, not unique): the address
   lowercased, without what follows the first `+` of the local part (unless nothing is left
   before it), and, on `gmail.com` and `googlemail.com`, without dots and with the domain
   `gmail.com`. Dots are dropped only on Gmail, where they are known not to matter; on other
   domains `a.na` and `ana` may be different people. A generated column fills the existing rows
   in its migration, needs no change where admins are inserted, and cannot drift from the e-mail.
   The domain states the same rule as `mailboxOf`, used by the in-memory fakes; an integration
   test checks the column against it for generated addresses.
2. **Aliases stay separate people.** The mailbox decides only the free plan: signing in, access
   to projects and e-mails still use the address as typed.
3. **One free place per mailbox.** A mailbox that already owns an account that is `free`, or
   unfinished and waiting for a free place, gets neither a place nor a place in the queue:
   - completing sign-up checks it under the advisory lock that counts the free places, so two
     aliases signing up at once never both take one; the account is born `incomplete`, waiting
     only for its subscription, and the answer says `free_place: taken_by_same_mailbox`;
   - opening places to the waiting accounts skips a mailbox that already has a free account and
     opens at most one account per mailbox, the one that waited longest;
   - falling back to the free plan when a subscription ends treats a free account of the same
     mailbox as the owner's other free account, so the account ends instead.
4. **The sign-up answer says what happened to the free place**, `free_place`: `placed`,
   `waitlisted`, `taken_by_same_mailbox` or `not_offered` (the free plan closed, or a returning
   owner given an account that already runs), so the dashboard does not tell a refused person to
   wait for a place.

## Consequences

- Someone who wants a second free account now needs a second real mailbox. Disposable mailboxes
  still work; Clause 6.4 and the abuse clause remain the answer to them.
- A paid account can still be opened with an alias of a mailbox on the free plan, which is what
  an agency measuring a client's site under its own inbox may want.
- Withdrawing a paid account within 7 days still puts it back on the free plan without looking
  at the mailbox: that return is promised by the terms as a right of withdrawal.
- Two different people whose provider gives meaning to `+` would share a mailbox and only one of
  them could be on the free plan; the other is offered the paid plan.
- A refused account is an unfinished one with no wait and no project, so the daily job removes
  it seven days after it was created, like any unfinished sign-up that never subscribes.
- The dashboard still decides from the accounts the signed-in admin owns whether cancelling a
  paid account goes back to the free plan. When another address of the same mailbox holds the
  free account, it promises the return and the API ends the account instead, until
  `GET /v1/me/accounts` says whether the mailbox has another free account and the dashboard
  reads it.
